# 1. Research foundations

Every scheduling rule in Rumbo traces back to one of the findings below. Where a rule is a design judgement rather than a research finding, the spec says so (marked **[design]**).

## Evidence → rule

| # | Finding | Key sources | Rule in Rumbo |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | A balanced course gives time to four strands: meaning-focused input, meaning-focused output, language-focused learning, and fluency development. | Nation (2007) | Every task belongs to a strand. The planner balances strands over a rolling 7 days, with targets that change by level. |
| R2 | Acquisition is driven by input you can understand. | Krashen (1982) | Input is the largest strand at every level (≈45%). |
| R3 | Comprehension needs high word coverage: ~95–98% for reading, perhaps ~90–95% for listening. | Hu & Nation (2000); Laufer & Ravenhorst-Kalovski (2010); van Zeeland & Schmitt (2013) | After each input session you rate comprehension. Rumbo nudges your Dreaming Spanish difficulty up or down to keep you in the "mostly understand" zone. |
| R4 | Producing language makes learners notice gaps; conscious noticing helps intake. | Swain (1985, 1995); Schmidt (1990) | Output starts at A2. Output tasks reuse language from that day's input ("use 3 sentences with X"). |
| R5 | Negotiating meaning with a real interlocutor supports acquisition. | Long (1996) | Tandem exchanges are a core output task, not an optional extra. |
| R6 | Speaking anxiety peaks in real-time interaction; planning time improves fluency and complexity. | Horwitz, Horwitz & Cope (1986); Yuan & Ellis (2003) | Speaking unlocks in stages: text → **planned** voice notes → live calls. |
| R7 | Spread-out practice beats massed practice; retrieval beats re-reading. | Cepeda et al. (2006, 2008); Roediger & Karpicke (2006) | Short daily sessions are fine. Duolingo is used as a retrieval warm-up. Your own mistakes become a spaced-repetition deck (FSRS scheduling). |
| R8 | Fluency grows by repeating easy material under time pressure. | Maurice (1983); Nation (1989) | Weekly 4/3/2 drill: tell the same story in 4, 3, then 2 minutes. Easy below-level videos count toward fluency. |
| R9 | Captions/subtitles help L2 listening comprehension and vocabulary. | Montero Perez, Van Den Noortgate & Desmet (2013) | Extra en Español is watched with Spanish subtitles on the first viewing at A2. |
| R10 | Shadowing improves listening and pronunciation, especially for lower-intermediate learners. | Hamada (2016) | A2–B1 intensive viewing includes a 2–3 minute shadowing step. |
| R11 | Written corrective feedback improves accuracy, especially when followed by revision. | Bitchener (2008); Kang & Han (2015) | Writing tasks: write → get AI feedback → **rewrite** → errors go into the mistake deck. |
| R12 | Habits take ~66 days on average to form, and a single missed day doesn't derail them. | Lally et al. (2010) | The weekly goal is the main metric. Streaks are lenient: earned freezes, a 10-minute day still counts. |
| R13 | Planning *when and where* ("if X, then I'll do Y") raises follow-through. | Gollwitzer (1999) | Onboarding asks you to pick a cue ("after breakfast", "on the bus"). Reminders use that cue. |
| R14 | Spanish is a Category I language: ~600–750 class hours to professional working proficiency for English speakers. | US Foreign Service Institute | Used for the long-term projection, alongside Dreaming Spanish's published hours roadmap (company guidance, not peer-reviewed). |
| R15 | Beginner Duolingo courses produce reading/listening outcomes comparable to several university semesters, but give little speaking practice. | Jiang et al. (2021) | Duolingo's share shrinks as the output strands grow, and drops out by B2. |

## Where Rumbo deliberately deviates

- **Input is weighted above Nation's 25%.** Nation's strands assume a classroom where much "output" time also contains input. Self-learners get far less incidental input, and learners at A2–B2 need thousands of word families (R3), so Rumbo gives input ≈45%. Fluency time also partly counts as easy input. **[design]**
- **Speaking earlier than Dreaming Spanish recommends.** Dreaming Spanish suggests delaying speaking until ~1,000 hours. R4–R6 support output much earlier, with anxiety handled by staging (R6) rather than by waiting.
- **Session order** (retrieval warm-up → new structure → input → output) is a design choice informed by R4/R7, not a tested sequence. **[design]**
